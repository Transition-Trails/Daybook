# Paste this into Replit — Wave 17 only

Wave 17 of four. This is the whole task; there is nothing to look up. Do not start any later wave.

**What this wave is.** The storefront is the first thing a tester will see, and it currently makes three
promises it does not keep. All three are front-of-house and none need new infrastructure. This should
land in one session.

**Out of scope, do not touch:** checkout, order delivery, the order or user schema, and everything under
`pages/studios/`. Nothing here changes how money moves or what the database holds.

## Step 1 — no priced card that cannot be bought

`artifacts/admin/src/pages/shop/StorefrontHome.tsx` renders the **Themes** and **Sticker packs**
sections as plain `<div>`s carrying a name and a price. Neither is a link — no `href`, no cursor
affordance, no detail route, no buy action. The **Editions** section directly above wraps every card in
a `Link`, so the page teaches a buyer that cards are clickable and then breaks that lesson twice. A
buyer is shown a theme marked `$4.00` and there is nothing to click.

**First, determine which of these is true**, then implement that branch and tell me which:

- **A theme or a sticker pack can be purchased server-side today.** There is a working code path for at
  least one of them (see finding D118 — the delivery format exists and one path works). If so: give each
  a detail route and a buy action that reuses **the same cart and checkout flow editions already use**.
  Do not build a parallel purchase path — one checkout, one cart.
- **It cannot yet.** If so: remove the price from those cards and render each section as a browsable
  showcase — name, swatches or tags, what is included — with no purchase affordance at all.

**The non-negotiable:** when this step is done there must be no card anywhere on the storefront that
displays a price and cannot be acted on. A browsable showcase with no price is honest. A priced dead
card is not.

## Step 2 — remove the "Sign in" link

The nav in the same file contains `<a href="/login">Sign in</a>`, which sends a customer to the Daybook
**admin console** login. There is no buyer account concept anywhere in the route tree, so the only
identity affordance on a public storefront points at a staff door — and a buyer who somehow satisfies it
has signed in as something they are not meant to be.

Delete the link. Do not replace it with anything, and do not build a buyer login in this wave. Wave 18
introduces buyer identity and restores this link pointing somewhere real.

## Step 3 — stop promising Drive before asking for it

The hero currently reads:

> Choose an edition, pick your style, and generate a personalised PDF planner — saved directly to your
> Drive.

That is the first sentence a buyer reads, and it commits the product to a Google Drive integration that
is never mentioned again and never consented to before purchase. It is also the only thing in that
sentence that can fail for reasons unrelated to the purchase itself.

1. **Soften the hero** so it promises the planner rather than the delivery mechanism. Drive is a genuine
   advantage, but it is an upgrade, not the headline.
2. **Introduce Drive where the buyer is already invested** — inside `shop/StoreBuilder.tsx`, at the point
   the planner is generated, as an optional "also save to Drive" with a plain one-line explanation of
   what connecting does. **If the buyer declines, or has no Google account, the purchase must complete
   normally and they must still get their file.** Drive must never become a step that can block a sale.

Match the copy already on the page: second person, sentence case, calm, concrete, no exclamation marks.
