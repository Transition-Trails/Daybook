# Wychcombe Atlas Visual Page System

**Companion to:** `wychcombe-illustrated-canon-story-atlas.md`  
**Prototype record:** Margaret Ashcroft  
**Format:** portrait US Letter pages presented as facing spreads  
**Status:** visual direction for review

## Design intention

The atlas should feel like a carefully maintained Victorian archive that has been opened for a modern reader. It must remain warm, botanical, and story-led without becoming a faux-antique scrapbook.

The page should reward two modes of reading:

- **Encounter:** illustration, title, hook, and narrative.
- **Study:** canon facts, relationships, questions, and source controls.

## Page geometry

| Element | Specification |

## Color system

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archive green | `#17382F` | primary headings, seals, rules |
| Deep ink | `#1F2E29` | body text |
| Antique ivory | `#F5F0E4` | page ground |
| Foxed cream | `#E7D8B9` | panels and captions |
| Weathered brass | `#AD8743` | folios, dividers, possibility frames |
| Moss | `#77816B` | secondary metadata |
| Sepia | `#735B43` | historical notes |
| Muted burgundy | `#744C47` | editorial questions and warnings |

The page background should be materially warm but quiet enough for long reading. Aging is use-based; no burned edges, theatrical stains, or uniform grunge.

## Typography

Preferred families should be locally embedded for final PDF output.

- **Display serif:** Spectral or Cormorant Garamond
- **Body serif:** Source Serif 4 or Literata
- **Metadata sans:** Instrument Sans or Source Sans 3
- **Record IDs:** IBM Plex Mono or Space Mono

Hierarchy:

- Part title: 25–30 pt serif
- Record title: 23–27 pt serif
- Story hook: 12–14 pt italic serif
- Section heading: 7.5–8.5 pt uppercase sans with tracking
- Body: 9.5–10.25 pt serif, 13–14 pt leading
- Metadata: 7–8 pt sans
- Canon ID: 7–8 pt mono

## Canon signals

Canon state must be visible without relying on color alone.

- **Accepted:** circular seal containing a check and “Accepted Canon.”
- **Proposed:** diamond seal containing “Under Consideration.”
- **Inference:** bracketed label, “The archive suggests.”
- **Editorial Question:** numbered burgundy margin card.
- **Story Possibility:** brass botanical frame and “Possibility—not yet canon.”
- **Unknown:** broken rule and “The record does not explain.”
- **Superseded:** struck seal plus replacement record.

## Master spread anatomy

### Page one — Encounter

1. Running head and part name
2. Canon seal and record ID
3. Record title
4. Story hook
5. Dominant illustration occupying approximately 55–65% of the page
6. Short narrative opening
7. Image caption and provenance ID
8. Folio

### Page two — Understanding

1. “The record tells us” narrative
2. “Why it matters”
3. Historical ground
4. Relationships
5. “The record does not explain”
6. Editorial questions
7. Tangible-object suggestions
8. Source strip with snapshot commit
9. Folio

## Illustration treatments

- Portraits should show work, choice, or relationship—not merely costume.
- Place art should establish movement, use, and social relationship.
- Object plates may use multiple views, measurements, annotations, and custody notes.
- Atmosphere plates may run full bleed as chapter transitions.
- Story maps are diagrams, not generated paintings; labels must remain exact.
- Every image requires an approved crop, print crop, alt text, rights status, linked canon ID, and provenance record.

## Botanical ornament

Botanical ornament should behave like archival evidence:

- fine specimen linework
- corner fragments rather than wreaths
- partial stems entering a margin
- catalog and register marks
- small brass dots or rules
- no generic floral bouquets

Use a different restrained botanical identifier for each major family or institution only after that association is approved.

## Sample-spread rationale: Margaret Ashcroft

Margaret is the correct first prototype because her record contains:

- developed narrative, history, visual notes, and editorial questions
- connections to both founding and second-generation material
- a strong visual motif: information moving from source material to printed circulation
- clear links to the Stationery House and future physical products
- meaningful unknowns that demonstrate how questions remain visible without weakening the narrative

The prototype uses only accepted Margaret material in its main voice. The Stationery House chronology and printing education appear as unresolved questions.

## Review checklist

- Does the spread feel like an illustrated history rather than a production specification?
- Can accepted canon be distinguished from questions and possibilities in grayscale?
- Does the illustration lead without reducing the record to a portrait?
- Is long-form reading comfortable at printed size?
- Are canon ID, status, snapshot, and source visible but subordinate?
- Do editorial questions feel inviting rather than like incomplete homework?
- Does the layout still work when the illustration changes orientation?
- Can the spread become a navigable digital PDF without changing its print composition?
