## 1. Editorial purpose

The atlas should make Wychcombe feel inhabited, remembered, and discoverable while preserving a clear boundary between fact, interpretation, and possibility.

It has four jobs:

1. **Bring the world to life** through art, narrative pacing, material culture, and visual relationships.
2. **Preserve canon accurately** with stable record IDs, statuses, source references, and snapshot provenance.
3. **Expose story potential** through questions, tensions, absences, objects, and relationships without silently converting them into canon.
4. **Connect story to experience** by identifying material that can become Daybook pages, stationery, artifacts, planner inserts, correspondence, maps, or future mysteries.

The atlas is not a database dump and not a conventional encyclopedia. It should read like a beautifully curated history assembled from records that do not always agree on what matters.

## 2. Canon presentation rules

Every statement in the atlas must belong to one of these treatments.

| Treatment | Meaning | Visual convention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accepted Canon** | Approved WorldSmith fact | Main narrative voice; dark green seal |
| **Proposed Canon** | Developed but not approved | Tinted border; “Under Consideration” heading |
| **Editorial Question** | Decision still required | Margin card with question-mark folio |
| **Story Possibility** | Evocative interpretation, not fact | Gold-bordered “Possibility” conservatory card |
| **Inference** | Logical reading across approved records | Labeled “The archive suggests…” with cited record IDs |
| **Unknown** | Deliberately absent or unresolved | “The record does not explain…” |
| **Superseded** | Retained for history but not current | Appendix only; struck seal and replacement reference |

No unlabeled speculative prose may appear in the main narrative.

### Recommended recurring labels

- The record tells us…
- Wychcombe remembers…
- The archive suggests…
- A Curator might know…
- The record does not explain…
- Questions for the storyteller
- Possibilities, not yet canon
- Objects that could make this tangible
- Where the reader may encounter this

## 4. Master record spread

Each full canon record uses a two-page spread when enough material exists.

### Left page — encounter

- Large illustration
- Record title
- Canon ID and status seal
- One-sentence story hook
- Category, period, and narrative visibility
- Short story-led introduction
- Optional pull quote derived from approved text

### Right page — understanding

- **The record tells us** — approved narrative summary
- **Why it matters** — function within Wychcombe
- **Historical ground** — concise historical context
- **Visual language** — setting, materials, light, clothing, objects, and exclusions
- **Relationships** — linked records with relationship labels
- **Story pressure** — what this record complicates or makes possible
- **The record does not explain** — genuine unknowns
- **Questions for the storyteller** — editorial decisions
- **Objects that could make this tangible** — Daybook, print, or physical artifact ideas
- **Sources** — record ID, linked records, snapshot commit

### Short-record treatment

Thin records should not be padded with invented prose. Use one of three formats:

- **Cabinet card:** one illustration, confirmed description, unknowns, and connections
- **Object plate:** front/back or detail views with custody, material, and symbolic notes
- **Constellation entry:** three to six related thin records shown together around a shared question

## 7. Knowledge layers

For every major record, identify what is known at five levels:

1. **Public knowledge** — what villagers, customers, or visitors may know.
2. **Family knowledge** — what the Ashcrofts and Bellamys understand.
3. **Curatorial knowledge** — what appears in controlled records.
4. **Private knowledge** — what one person may conceal or never have recorded.
5. **Lost or disputed knowledge** — what survives only through gaps, objects, or conflicting memory.

These layers will make future mysteries possible without retroactively making every character implausibly uninformed.

## 8. Editorial question framework

Questions should be sorted by consequence rather than curiosity.

### Foundation questions

Answers change many records and should be resolved first.

- What were the legal and financial circumstances by which Elias gained control of the property?
- Who owned it before him?
- When did “Wychcombe” begin to refer to the estate/institution as well as the village?
- Was the stewardship principle ever legally formalized?
- What are the Curators: title, profession, institution, calling, or combination?
- Who appoints a Curator and what authority does the role carry?

### Relationship questions

- Did James explicitly support Margaret’s succession?
- What role did Clara play in Elias’s decision?
- What did Cecily want from Wychcombe?
- How did Thomas Vale’s relationship with the second generation differ by child?
- Did the village view Margaret’s Stationery House as service, commerce, intrusion, or all three?

### Material questions

- What exactly did the old press and type cases look like?
- What survives from the building’s pre-1884 printing history?
- Which Stationery House publications became recognizable Wychcombe objects?
- Who made, numbered, held, or dispersed the Seven Keys?
- What physical evidence distinguishes an Ashcroft record, Bellamy record, and Curatorial record?

### Mystery questions

- Which absence was accidental, and which was made?
- Can a record be authentic but misleading?
- Who benefits when access appears to be governed by tradition?
- Which object’s accepted meaning rests on an assumption rather than proof?
- What did the village remember that the archive chose not to record?

- canon source
- readable-text permissions

## 11. Build and refresh process

1. Read the latest `context-snapshots` branch.
2. Record the exact commit SHA.
3. Inventory records by category and status.
4. Detect additions, removals, status changes, and altered text since the previous atlas build.
5. Update the editorial question register.
6. Match approved illustrations to canon IDs.
7. Generate the atlas draft.
8. Run canon-boundary review.
9. Run visual and print review.
10. Publish the PDF with a source manifest and edition number.

A build should never silently absorb changes. Material changes require a new atlas edition or revision entry.

---

## 12. First build definition of done

The Founding Circle prototype is ready when it contains:

- cover, reading key, and source manifest
- the five-part narrative through Stewardship and the Curators
- at least eight fully illustrated major records
- four story maps
- one chronology
- accepted/proposed/possible treatments applied consistently
- editorial question register
- artifact-extension suggestions
- complete canon index
- illustration provenance
- exact source commit
- screen and print inspection

The first prototype may use labeled image placeholders. Missing artwork must not block testing the narrative architecture.
