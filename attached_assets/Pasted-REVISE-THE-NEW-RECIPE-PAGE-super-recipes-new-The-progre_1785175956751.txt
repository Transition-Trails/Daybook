=== 1. ADD STEP 0 — "✦ START WITH CLAUDE" ===

Header: a 32px clay-gradient ✦ avatar, then the eyebrow "START WITH CLAUDE" and

Body: a textarea for the description (placeholder e.g. "A mobile-sized planner —

a secondary "Start over" button appears beside it that clears the staged state.

ON SUBMIT, Claude must do three things — all three matter:

  (a) STAGE THE RECIPE. Set the product type in Step 1, toggle the parts in Step 2,
      and fill the decision card in Step 3. Everything stays editable — this is a
      starting point, not a commit. Show a green pill reading
      "STAGED BELOW — ADJUST ANYTHING".

  (b) EXPLAIN ITS READING, as a labelled list titled "What Claude read from that":
        TYPE      — why this product type
        PARTS ON  — which engines it enabled
        PARTS OFF — which it deliberately left off AND WHY (the exclusions are the
                    valuable part; do not omit them)
        QUESTION  — why this is the buyer's first either/or
      Worked example for the mobile-planner prompt:
        Type      "Planner — it is dated, so the date and hyperlink engines apply."
        Parts on  "Page recipes, date engine, hyperlink layer, trackers."
        Parts off "Tab rail — edge tabs do not work at phone width; a top nav bar
                   reads better. That is the gap below."
        Question  "Dated or undated, same as any planner. Nothing about mobile
                   changes what the buyer decides first."

  (c) FLAG ENGINE GAPS — THE MOST IMPORTANT BEHAVIOUR. When the described product
      needs capability the engines do not have, Claude must say so explicitly
      rather than staging something that cannot generate. Render these in a warm
      warning treatment (bg #FDF6EF, border #F0E0CF) under the heading
      "⚠ This recipe needs engine work first", each with a title, a concrete
      explanation, and a severity pill:
        "Blocks release"   (bg #FBECEB, text #A33A32)
        "Needs a decision" (bg #FDF0E6, text var(--ppp-cocoa))
      Worked example:
        Page size profile · Blocks release — "The page engine has A5, B6, Personal,
          Half letter, Letter and iPad 4:3. There is no phone trim. A mobile recipe
          needs a new profile — roughly 1170 × 2532 at 3:1 aspect — before this can
          generate."
        Tab rail at narrow width · Needs a decision — "Edge tabs need about 40mm of
          margin. At phone width that is a third of the page. Either ship without
          tabs or add a top-bar nav variant to the tab engine."
      Close with: "Claude staged this so you can review it, but it cannot generate
      until the engine work above ships. Save it as a draft, build the profile,
      then release."

  Gap detection must be REAL, not a fixed list: compare what the description implies
  (page trim, binding, link behaviour, cut paths, print path) against the engine
  capabilities that actually exist in the codebase, and report genuine mismatches.
  A recipe carrying an unresolved "Blocks release" gap may be SAVED AS A DRAFT but
  must NOT be publishable — enforce that server-side, not just in the UI.

  Reuse the existing grounded Claude plumbing (store-profile grounding, aiEnabled
  gating, audit logging). Do not build a second chat implementation.

=== 2. CLARIFY THE RIGHT RAIL ===
The three numbers are three DIFFERENT UNITS shown identically, which reads as an
unlabelled score. Give each number its unit inline, stacked (label / number+unit /
description), and retitle the rail.

  Rail title:    "How many choices each person faces"
  Rail subtitle: "The same product, seen from three sides. Choice narrows at every
                  level — that is the whole design."

  You · platform  →  [N] parts offered
                     "Every part this product type can use. You decide which of them
                      the recipe turns on."
  Store owner     →  [N] parts they can touch
                     "Only the ones you left on above — and already filled in by
                      their theme. They adjust; they do not assemble."
  Consumer        →  [3] choices at checkout
                     "The buyer question, a theme, and a palette. Everything else
                      was decided before they arrived."

  The consumer number is NOT a part count — it is decisions. Labelling it as such is
  the point of the change. Keep the en-dash in each number until a type is picked.
