name: WorldSmith readable-text governance
description: Durable rules for closed-world readable content in image-provider prompts.
---

When inherited policy prohibits invented readable text, every requested string is closed-world: only exact wording explicitly authorized by the Production Specification or a Canon Record whose normalized status is exactly `Accepted` may render. Unspecified text-bearing areas become blank or non-readable period-appropriate marks, and authorization includes stable source identity plus field provenance.

**Why:** Image prompts can turn contextual names, dates, reference phrases, or factual-looking ephemera into invented readable claims. Heading-based trust, broad negative-clause exemptions, fuzzy status matching, and embedded participles such as “a plate engraved [name]” create provider-boundary bypasses even when the visible governance lock looks correct.

**How to apply:** Treat every provider-bound readable request as closed-world, including mixed negative/affirmative phrasing and text directives embedded in descriptive prose. Only demonstrably pure prohibitions may avoid authorization. Do not classify technical noun phrases such as “print requirements,” quoted decorative adjectives, or narrative past-tense phrases such as “demanded to be written down” and “organized, printed, and shared” as requested copy; quoted text still requires a text-bearing or directive context. If inherited material exceeds a provider limit, compact source-detail sections but preserve the canonical readable-governance and negative-prompt sections. Canonical-section verification and removal must tolerate whitespace normalization and verified truncation markers while still scanning any appended remainder or duplicate governance section; avoid regex construction from unbounded prompt text.
