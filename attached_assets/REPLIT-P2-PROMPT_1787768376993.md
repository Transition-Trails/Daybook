Everything you need is in this file. Do not look for other briefs.

**Context.** The PDF generator's calendar defects were just fixed: `getMonthGridOffset(year, month, weekStart)`
now feeds all three call sites, the week-start backward walk reaches the boundary, and
`test/calendar-correctness.test.ts` pins both. Verified independently — 1 Jan 2027 is a Friday, 1 Aug 2027 is

**This wave** produces real 2027 planners the owner can inspect by eye, plus a script that refuses to emit a
broken set. Tests prove a formula; they cannot tell her a planner is good. Same shape as the cut-line proof:
the machine checks everything checkable, the human step is only judgement.

## Step 1 — close the one gap the calendar suite left

The suite covers three hand-verified month anchors, the week-start walk, weekly coverage, a year boundary,
link alignment and calendar URIs. Choosing hand-verified anchors over a derived twelve-month loop was the
right call — a loop built from the same formula would only restate it. Keep that approach.

One case is missing: **a 29-day February.** Add to `artifacts/api-server/src/test/calendar-correctness.test.ts`:

- `getMonthGridOffset(2028, 1, "sun")` and `(2028, 1, "mon")`, with the weekday hand-verified in a comment.
- A generated February 2028 month grid where **day 29 exists and lands in the correct slot.**

An off-by-one that only appears in a leap February is exactly the kind that ships.

## Step 3 — refuse to emit a broken proof set

Exit non-zero with a message naming the problem if any of these fail. A bad artifact that reaches the
inspection step wastes the expensive input, which is her time.

1. **Every internal link resolves.** No hyperlink targets a page that does not exist.
2. **No duplicate page ids,** and every id in the page map appears exactly once in the document.
3. **The 1st of every month sits in the slot its weekday requires.** Check this by reading the generated
   document — **not** by calling `getMonthGridOffset` again. Calling the function under test to verify its own
   output proves nothing.
4. **The Monday and Sunday files differ,** and every week page starts on the correct weekday.
5. **The 13-month file spans into January 2028** and contains day pages for 31 Dec 2027 and 1 Jan 2028.
6. **Page count is within a plausible band** for the section list, and all four page counts are printed so a
   wild number is immediately visible.

**Assertion 1 is the most valuable thing in this wave.** Dead hyperlinks that look live are completely
invisible to visual inspection — a planner can look flawless and have a hundred buttons going nowhere. If that
assertion is the only one you get right, running this was worth it.

Print a one-screen summary on success: page count, link count, dead-link count (zero), and the first-of-month
slot for all twelve months of each file.

## Step 4 — the inspection checklist

Add `docs/planner-2027-verification.md`, following the shape of `docs/cutline-proof-verification.md`:
procedure first, then an empty results table. What to look at, one line each, in this order:

- **Cover** — year, edition name, theme applied, and the week-start claim matches the filename.
- **A month spread** — pick March. The 1st is under the right weekday, the grid is not crowded, day numbers
  are legible, and trailing empty cells look intentional rather than broken.
- **A week spread** — seven day columns, correct dates, and the first and last weekly pages of the year do
  not show controls that lead nowhere.
- **A day page** — date correct, sections present, and any calendar link opens the right date.
- **Navigation round-trip** — from the month grid, tap into a day, back out, then forward a week. This is the
  journey a buyer actually performs and no test simulates it.
- **Typography and density** at 100% on a tablet-sized view, since that is where these are used.

Include fields for date, device, viewer app (GoodNotes / Notability / Noteshelf), theme, and who ran it.
**Leave the results table empty** — filling it in is the human step and it is not yours.

## Rules

## Definition of done

`pnpm proof:planner` produces the four 2027 PDFs and exits zero with the summary printed; all six assertions
in step 3 hold; the leap-February test is added and passing; `docs/planner-2027-verification.md` exists with an
empty results table.
