# Daybook — D124: prove the cut line on a real machine

**One finding, one deliverable.** Wave 10 closed D41–D44 and shipped four fixtures, so the bytes we
emit are verified. Nothing proves that Design Space opens the file and the blade follows the art —
which is the claim the product makes to a seller. This task builds the proof kit so that claim can be
tested once, on one sheet, and the result written down.

**Scope discipline:** this is a proof harness only. Do **not** modify `lib/imageProcessing.ts`, the
sticker routes, the schema, or the four checked-in fixtures. If the harness reveals a pipeline defect,
stop and report it — do not fix it in this task.

---

## Step 1 — one script that builds the proof sheet

Add `scripts/src/build-cutline-proof.ts`, runnable as a package script (`pnpm proof:cutline` or the
repo's existing convention). It takes no arguments and writes into `proof/` (gitignored).

**It must call the real pipeline.** Import `dilateAlpha`, `resolveBorderWidthMm` and
`generateCutlineSvg` from `artifacts/api-server/src/lib/imageProcessing.ts` and drive them through the
same call sequence a sticker route uses. Do not reimplement the tracing, the dilation, or the shadow
padding — a proof of a reimplementation proves nothing.

**Exercise the 300-DPI path, not the legacy one.** Per `.agents/memory/sticker-cutline-physical-scale.md`,
96 DPI is for unsized legacy artwork and 300 DPI only applies after a `sizeInMm` render. Sellers get
sized stickers, so every fixture in this sheet goes through a `sizeInMm` render first and the cutline
generator receives 300 as its effective DPI. Rasterize the four fixture SVGs with the resvg the repo
already uses for spec boards — no new dependencies.

**Sizes.** Declare these explicitly in the script as named constants, because they are what gets
measured with a ruler:

| Fixture | Rendered size | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `plain.svg` | 38.0 mm wide | one closed cut around one silhouette |
| `bordered.svg` | 38.0 mm wide | D41 — the cut follows the art, not the page edge |
| `two-part.svg` | 60.0 mm wide | D42 — two disjoint blobs become **two separate cuts** |
| `holed.svg` | 38.0 mm wide | D42 — the interior hole is cut as a hole (`fill-rule: evenodd`) |

Border width: 2.0 mm on `bordered.svg`, 0 on the rest. Pass it as millimetres so
`resolveBorderWidthMm` takes its mm path rather than its 96-DPI legacy conversion.

**Two output files, one shared origin:**

- `proof/cutline-proof-print.png` — US Letter at 300 DPI, exactly 2550 × 3300 px.
- `proof/cutline-proof-cut.svg` — US Letter declared in **millimetres** (215.9 × 279.4 mm), containing
  only the cut paths.

The print PNG and the cut SVG must be laid out from the same coordinate origin and the same
millimetre grid, so a misalignment on the cutting mat is a real finding rather than a harness bug.

**Put a calibration square on the sheet.** A 50.0 mm filled square in the top-left margin, labelled
`50.0 mm` in printed text, in the PNG only — never in the cut SVG. If the printed square does not
measure 50 mm, the printer scaled the page and nothing downstream is meaningful. This is the first
thing measured and it takes ten seconds.

**Label each sticker on the print.** Under each one, print its fixture name and its expected measured
width (`two-part.svg · 60.0 mm`). The person holding the ruler should not have to consult a document
to know what the number should be.

## Step 2 — refuse to emit a broken proof sheet

The script fails loudly, with a non-zero exit and a message naming the problem, if any of these do not
hold. A bad sheet that gets printed and cut wastes the one hour this task is trying to spend well.

1. The PNG is exactly 2550 × 3300 px.
2. The SVG's `width` / `height` are declared in `mm` and equal 215.9 × 279.4 — not px, not unitless.
3. `plain`, `bordered` and `holed` each contribute **one** outer closed subpath; `two-part` contributes
   **two**. `holed` contributes one additional interior subpath and its path carries
   `fill-rule="evenodd"`.
4. No cut path intersects the calibration square's area, and no cut path is within 6 mm of the sheet
   edge.
5. Every fixture's cut-path bounding box matches its declared width from the table above within
   ±0.4 mm. This is the assertion that catches a DPI mix-up before it reaches vinyl.

Print a one-screen summary of the measured bounding boxes and subpath counts on success, so the
numbers are visible without opening the files.

## Step 3 — the record sheet

Add `docs/cutline-proof-verification.md`, following the shape of the existing
`docs/google-reconnect-verification.md`. It holds the procedure and an empty results table to fill in
by hand:

- Print `cutline-proof-print.png` at 100% / actual size, scaling off. Note the printer and paper.
- Measure the calibration square. **If it is not 50.0 mm ±0.5, stop** — fix the print settings and
  reprint before cutting.
- Upload `cutline-proof-cut.svg` to Design Space. Record whether it imports as a cut file without
  manual intervention, and whether the holed sticker's interior appears as a cut rather than a fill.
- Cut on vinyl. Record: blade offset by more than 0.5 mm anywhere · the two-part sticker cutting as
  one shape instead of two · the hole not cut · the bordered sticker cut on the art instead of the
  2 mm border · any measured width off by more than 0.5 mm.
- A results table with a row per fixture: expected width, measured width, subpaths expected, subpaths
  cut, pass/fail, notes. Plus fields for date, machine, blade, material and who ran it.

Leave the table empty. Filling it in is the physical step and it is not yours.

---

## Definition of done

`pnpm proof:cutline` produces both files and exits zero with the summary printed; the assertions in
step 2 all hold; `docs/cutline-proof-verification.md` exists with an empty results table.
`lib/imageProcessing.ts`, the sticker routes and the four fixtures are untouched — confirm that in
your report.

Report back: the printed summary of measured bounding boxes and subpath counts, and anything the
assertions caught along the way. If assertion 5 failed at any point during development, say so and say
why — a DPI or unit mistake found here is a real finding about the pipeline, not a harness detail.
