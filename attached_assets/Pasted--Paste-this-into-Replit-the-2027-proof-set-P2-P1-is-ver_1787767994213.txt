## What this wave is

Real 2027 planners the owner can inspect by eye, plus a script that refuses to emit a broken set. Tests
prove the formula; they cannot tell her the planner is good.

Four PDFs — Monday vertical, Sunday vertical, Monday landscape, and a 13-month Monday file — generated
through the **real** `buildPdf`, with six fail-closed assertions and a printed summary. Then a human
checklist in `docs/planner-2027-verification.md`, table left empty.

## The two things that matter most

**Assertion 1 is the valuable one: every internal link must resolve.** Dead hyperlinks that look live are
the D12 and D15 family, and they are completely invisible to visual inspection — a planner can look
perfect and have a hundred buttons going nowhere. If that assertion is the only one you get right, the
wave was worth running.

**Assertion 3 must be independent.** Check the 1st-of-month placement by reading the generated document,
not by calling `getMonthGridOffset` again. Calling the function under test to verify its own output proves
nothing.

## One gap P1 left

The suite has no 29-day February. Step 1 adds it: `getMonthGridOffset(2028, 1, …)` for both week starts
with a hand-verified comment, and a generated February 2028 grid where day 29 exists in the right slot. An
off-by-one that only shows up in a leap February is exactly the kind that ships.

Choosing hand-verified anchors over a derived loop in P1 was the right call, by the way — a loop built from
the same formula would only restate it. Keep that approach.
